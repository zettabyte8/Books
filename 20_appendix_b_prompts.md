# Appendix B: Discussion and Essay Prompts

**Foundations**
1. Chapter 2 argues there is no neutral choice architecture. Does that make every designer a persuader? Where does design end and influence begin?
2. Cialdini's seven principles are described as useful rather than honest. Can a lever be both at once? Use one concrete example.
3. Chapter 4 works the Overton window forward on marriage equality and backward on carbon pricing. Which of the three moves dominates each, and is the carbon case a shift or a collapse?
4. Chapter 4 claims harm is often success's residue. Find a case in your own life and test the claim against it.

**Cases**
5. Compare Speer's Cathedral of Light with a modern stadium light show. What does each assume about its audience?
6. Speer's memoir supplies our standard description of the Cathedral of Light. Which parts of Chapter 6 would survive if the memoir were discredited entirely?
7. Lysenkoism failed on evidence. Tobacco agnotology succeeded by *supplying* evidence. Why did one strategy fail and the other work?
8. Chapter 8 sets RTLM against the British MOI. Which variable mattered most, and could RTLM have acquired it?
9. Straus argues radio's role in Rwanda is overstated. If he is right, what is the case for prosecuting broadcasters at all?
10. Chapter 9 ranks three patronage arrangements. Rank them by how easily a citizen can resist each, and defend the ranking.
11. The 2017 Qatar hack produced a blockade in days. Name one thing Chapters 7 and 8 prepare you to notice about it, and one thing they do not.
12. Chapter 10 says the platform patches and the state watches. Which of the two should be doing which, and what could a regulator actually reach?
13. Chapter 11 calls written interference "litigable." Draft the editorial-independence clause you would put in a public broadcaster's charter, and predict how it fails.
14. Chapter 12 proposes a concept rather than reporting one. Set out the strongest case against compression, then the strongest for it. Which side has the evidence?

**Now**
15. Kalla and Broockman found personality targeting no better than demographics. Should firms be required to disclose targeting criteria if targeting barely works?
16. Apply TARES to your own school's communications and grade it. Present findings to the person who writes them.
17. Chapter 15 concedes that three of its four levers are European in reach. Using Chapters 10 to 12, describe what a regulator outside the EU actually does instead.
18. Argue for or against constitutional neurorights. You must address enforcement cost.

**Synthesis**
19. The first edition called RTLM a "primary instrument" two sentences after citing a scholar who disputes it. Find three more places where a confident sentence sits on top of an uncertain source. What does that pattern suggest about machine-written nonfiction?
20. The first edition was entirely European and American; this one has four non-Atlantic chapters. Is four enough, and what would a fifth have to cover to avoid becoming a survey?
21. Chapter 14 says literacy is necessary and insufficient. Chapter 15 says law is slow, costly, and parochial. What is left, and is it enough?
