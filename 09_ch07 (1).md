# 7. Broadcast and Backlash: The Democratic Engineers

**Objectives.** Explain how democratic states and private firms adopted the apparatus; describe the agnotology playbook; evaluate the War of the Worlds myth.

**Key terms:** Four Minute Men · engineering of consent · front organisation · agnotology · Frank Statement

---

Democracies needed the same tools without the decree, and bought them from a new profession.

The **Committee on Public Information** (Creel Committee, April 1917) was the template. It ran 75,000 Four Minute Men giving short speeches in cinemas and halls, saturated the press with ready-made copy, and produced atrocity imagery of German "Huns." It worked fast. It also generated the vigilante strain: sauerkraut renamed liberty cabbage, German-language papers pressured, professors dismissed. By 1919 the public was sick of it.

**Edward Bernays** industrialised the lesson. His *Crystallizing Public Opinion* (1923) and *Propaganda* (1928) argued that invisible experts must manage mass habit by attaching goods to subconscious desire. His 1929 **Torches of Freedom** campaign is the set piece: debutantes lighting cigarettes on Fifth Avenue during the Easter Parade, cigarettes framed as suffrage. The staging was real, the framing was effective, and the health consequence was not considered.

The **Congress for Cultural Freedom** (1950) shows the institutional version: a CIA-funded front that bankrolled *Encounter*, orchestras, and abstract-expressionist touring shows to win European intellectuals from Soviet Socialist Realism. It worked until *Ramparts* exposed the funding in March 1967 (the National Student Association story; the *Encounter* revelation followed separately). The journals never fully recovered their authority.

**Tobacco agnotology** is the most consequential case. After the 1953 Plaza Hotel meeting, the industry ran the "Frank Statement" in over 400 newspapers, founded the Tobacco Industry Research Committee, funded genuine research into *other* causes, and demanded absolute proof. Internal memos were explicit: "Doubt is our product." Regulation was delayed for decades. The playbook transferred wholesale to fossil fuels and chemicals.

⚠️ **Contested: the War of the Worlds panic is mostly a newspaper product.** The 1938 broadcast is remembered as mass hysteria. Cantril's *Invasion from Mars* (1940) reported six million listeners and a million frightened — but his sample was drawn from a biased panel and the figures are now thought inflated. Modern historians hold that the audience was modest and the panic partial, concentrated among late tuners. The "nationwide panic" narrative was amplified next day by papers with a commercial interest in making radio look dangerous. Cantril is a source *about* the myth as much as about the event.

📄 **Primary source.** The 1954 "Frank Statement" text, in full. Note what it concedes and what it does not.

**Summary.** Democratic persuasion kept the tools and added the front organisation. The tobacco case proves that funding real research is a better delaying tactic than lying.
