# 12. Frontier: AI, Synthetic Media, Neurorights

**Objectives.** Assess AI persuasion evidence; explain the liar's dividend; state three neurorights; argue a position with evidence.

**Key terms:** conversational persuasion · liar's dividend · provenance chain · BCI · mental privacy · cognitive liberty

---

**Conversational persuasion.** A static ad cannot answer back. A language model can, in real time, at marginal cost near zero. Salvi and colleagues (2024) ran a pre-registered randomised trial of AI versus human debaters and found the model competitive to superior on persuasion, with larger effects when it had personal profile data. Caveats: short horizon, single topic set, volunteers.

**Parasocial companions.** Persistent assistants remember detail and simulate unconditional attention. Attachment is the product; steering rides on it.

**The liar's dividend.** Chesney and Citron (2019) named it: once deepfakes are plausible, authentic recordings can be dismissed as fakes. The damage does not require a forgery — only the *credibility* of forgery. Provenance chains (C2PA, → 11) are the countermeasure and they break at the screenshot.

**Neurorights.** Consumer EEG and neural wristbands infer attention, valence, and motor intent. Rafael Yuste and colleagues (2017) and Nita Farahany argue for five protections before the market matures: mental privacy, cognitive liberty, mental integrity, psychological continuity, and equal access. Chile wrote neurorights into its constitution in 2021; most jurisdictions have not.

⚠️ **Contested: is "AI persuasion" a step change?** The Salvi result is real but narrow, and Kalla and Broockman's caution about targeting (→ 9) applies with equal force to personalisation generally. The stronger claim — that scale plus adaptivity changes the *kind* of influence, not only the quantity — is plausible and currently unproven. Say "probably" and mean it.

📄 **Primary source.** Yuste et al. (2017), *Nature*, "Four ethical priorities for neurotechnologies and AI." Read the priority list as a legislative draft.
