# 10. The Machine: Computational Persuasion

**Objectives.** Analyse six computational techniques; evaluate the Cambridge Analytica claim against its rebuttal; explain filter-bubble critique.

**Key terms:** engagement optimisation · microtargeting · dark ad · filter bubble · bot swarm · epistemic flooding · dark pattern

---

Broadcast pushed one message at everyone. Networks push a different message at each person, continuously, and adjust on the response. Six techniques carry the load.

**1. Engagement optimisation.** Ranking models are trained to maximise clicks, dwell, and shares. High-arousal moral emotion — anger, indignation, disgust — reliably outperforms nuance (Brady et al., 2017). Nobody chose polarisation; it fell out of the proxy (→ 4).

**2. Psychographic microtargeting.** Behavioural exhaust — likes, dwell time, location pings, purchase history — is used to infer traits, then to serve "dark ads" visible only to the target segment. The public sphere fractures: two neighbours can be told contradictory things and neither can see it.

**3. Filter bubbles and echo chambers.** Collaborative filtering recommends the similar. Pariser named the bubble in 2011.

**4. Bot swarms and synthetic consensus.** Automated and semi-automated accounts manufacture the appearance of a majority, exploiting social proof at scale.

**5. Epistemic flooding.** Volume and contradiction over coherence — the "firehose of falsehood." The aim is not to convince but to exhaust.

**6. Dark patterns.** Interface traps: roach-motel cancellation, confirmshaming, disguised ads, infinite scroll on a variable-reward schedule.

⚠️ **Contested: Cambridge Analytica.** The 2018 story — 87 million profiles, OCEAN-based psychographic targeting, Brexit and 2016 swung by personality-matched ads — is the most cited case in the field. Kalla and Broockman (2020) ran the first large pre-registered tests and found personality-targeted ads performed *no better* than ordinary demographic targeting. Their conclusion: the technique works, the mystique is oversold. Treat CA as a case about data acquisition and disclosure, not about persuasive magic.

⚠️ **Contested: how closed are the bubbles?** Early claims were strong. Later panel-data work (Guess and colleagues, 2018–2023) found most people's news diets are *less* echo-chambered than assumed, and that exposure to opposing views is higher than the theory predicts. The mechanism is real; the prevalence is smaller. Selection effects and platform differences explain much of the variation.

📄 **Primary source.** Kalla & Broockman (2020), abstract and design section. Note what they pre-registered and why that matters.
