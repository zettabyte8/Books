## 2. Overview

**Title:** The Architecture of Consent and Control
**Subtitle:** How Built Space, Information Systems, and Institutions Steer Human Behaviour — and How to Steer Yourself Back
**Length:** 9,000 words core (complete); 50,000 at trade length
**Illustrations:** 4 commissioned plates (cover + 3 part dividers), supplied
**Category:** Social psychology / media studies / civics education
**Audience:** ages 15+, secondary and post-secondary classrooms, public libraries, civic programmes

The book's thesis is that influence is designed into four registers — physical space, information systems, interface, and institutional rule — and that the four compound. The Creel Committee, the Nuremberg Cathedral of Light, the pre-ticked consent box, and the ad library are all one machine with different parts. Analysts who look at one register under-report the pressure, which is why the book teaches all four and then teaches the reader to audit them.

Part I builds the toolkit. Part II runs it across five centuries. Part III turns it on the present and hands the reader SIFT, TARES, and the publicity principle.

## 3. Market and positioning

The appetite is documented: *Merchants of Doubt* (Oreskes & Conway, 2010) sold through a decade of climate denial; *Foolproof* (van der Linden, 2023) made inoculation a trade topic; Finland's national media-literacy curriculum is now cited in policy papers on three continents. What is missing is a **classroom-shaped** book. The existing titles are written for adults who already read nonfiction; teachers adapting them write their own objectives and activities.

**Comps and the difference:**

| Comp | What it does | What we do differently |
|---|---|---|
| *Merchants of Doubt* | Narrative history of agnotology | Adds mechanism, cases beyond tobacco, and exercises |
| *Foolproof* | Inoculation, popular science | Adds law, institutions, and a full scheme of work |
| *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* | Structural critique, dense | Half the length, teachable, ends in practice not despair |

**Gap filled:** a text a teacher can open at Chapter 10 and run tomorrow.

## 4. Structure

Part I — Foundations: 1 What Mass Influence Is · 2 Four Architectures · 3 The Cognitive Toolkit · 4 Why Campaigns Fail
Part II — Cases: 5 Ink · 6 Concrete · 7 Broadcast and Backlash · 8 Radio and the Limits of Causation
Part III — Now: 9 The Machine · 10 Defence · 11 Law, Institutions, Ethics · 12 Frontier
Appendices A–E: Glossary · Prompts · Annotated History · Sources · Scheme of Work and Answer Key

## 5. Pedagogical apparatus

Every chapter: 3 objectives (copyable into a lesson plan), key terms, body text, primary-source box, Contested box where relevant, 3 comprehension checks, 1 activity, summary, cross-reference. Appendix E supplies a six-session route, a full-term route, an answer key, two rubrics, and adaptation notes for ages 13–14 and for undergraduates. Reading level is held at roughly grade 10: mean sentence length is a third shorter than the source draft.

## 6. AI collaboration disclosure

Drafting model: Gemini (Google). Audit and rewrite model: Venice AI. Direction, prompt design, and editorial control: Thomas G. Coleman.

The disclosure is structural, not decorative. Appendix C prints all fifteen prompts with an annotation of what each one actually did — including the finding that seven of fifteen were about tooling failures rather than content, and that the book's thin pedagogy was a direct consequence of nobody specifying an audience. That record is the most teachable artefact in the book, and no human-authored text can offer it.

Nothing is claimed as human-written that was not. Nothing generated is presented unverified: the bibliography is flagged for physical verification before print, and Chapters 6 and 8 are marked for specialist review.
