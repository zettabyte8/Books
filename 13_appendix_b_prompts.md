# Appendix B: Discussion and Essay Prompts

**Foundations**
1. Chapter 2 argues there is no neutral choice architecture. Does that make every designer a persuader? Where does design end and influence begin?
2. Cialdini's seven principles are described as useful rather than honest. Can a lever be both at once? Use one concrete example.
3. Chapter 4 claims harm is often success's residue. Find a case in your own life and test the claim against it.

**Cases**
4. Compare Speer's Cathedral of Light with a modern stadium light show. What does each assume about its audience?
5. Lysenkoism failed on evidence. Tobacco agnotology succeeded by *supplying* evidence. Why did one strategy fail and the other work?
6. Chapter 8 sets RTLM against the British MOI. Which variable mattered most, and could RTLM have acquired it?
7. Straus argues radio's role in Rwanda is overstated. If he is right, what is the case for prosecuting broadcasters at all?

**Now**
8. Kalla and Broockman found personality targeting no better than demographics. Should firms be required to disclose targeting criteria if targeting barely works?
9. Apply TARES to your own school's communications and grade it. Present findings to the person who writes them.
10. Argue for or against constitutional neurorights. You must address enforcement cost.

**Synthesis**
11. The first edition of this book called RTLM a "primary instrument" two sentences after citing a scholar who disputes it. Find three more places where a confident sentence sits on top of an uncertain source. What does that pattern suggest about machine-written nonfiction?
12. Chapter 10 says literacy is necessary and insufficient. Chapter 11 says law is slow and costly. What is left, and is it enough?
