# 6. Concrete: The Totalitarian Laboratory

**Objectives.** Describe the four institutions of totalitarian propaganda; explain the Cathedral of Light; distinguish agitation from integration propaganda; assess Lysenkoism as a case of ideology over evidence.

**Key terms:** agitprop · Reich Ministry of Public Enlightenment · integration propaganda · cult of personality · Lysenkoism

---

The 20th century ran the experiment at full power, and the results are the clearest evidence the light/dark distinction has any grip.

The **Committee of Union and Progress** in the late Ottoman Empire pioneered the modern apparatus: a party organ, a youth league, telegraph control, and a systematic cult of the leader. The **Bolsheviks** built it out: *agitprop* departments, the Agitprop train, ROSTA windows of stencilled posters, and the wholesale absorption of the press.

The **Reich Ministry of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda** (RMVP, March 1933, under Goebbels) is the textbook instance. Note the name: not "information" but *enlightenment*, a claim to be doing education. The Ministry controlled press, film, radio, theatre, music, and literature through the Reich Chamber of Culture, which licensed practitioners and thereby made compliance a condition of employment. The 1933 book burnings were the visible edge of an index; the invisible edge was that a writer who resigned from the Chamber could not be published at all.

**Speer's Cathedral of Light** (Nuremberg, 1934–37) is the case this book's title exists for. 130 searchlights at 8 km intervals threw vertical columns into the night, enclosing 150,000 people in an implied building with no roof. It was cheap, temporary, and overwhelming: architecture that made a rally feel like a liturgy. Compare it to the panopticon (→ 2) — one uses presence, the other the mere promise of it.

**Lysenkoism** is the epistemic case. Trofim Lysenko's rejection of Mendelian genetics was ideologically congenial, so it was installed by decree, its critics purged, and Soviet biology frozen for a generation. Lysenko promised harvests that did not arrive. The damage extended to China, where Michurinism was adopted alongside the separate sparrow-extermination campaign of 1958 — two distinct policies, often conflated, both contributing to the Great Leap famine.

⚠️ **Contested: was it all coercion?** Jacques Ellul (*Propaganda*, 1962) argued that *integration* propaganda — the continuous bath of film, education, and journalism — does the durable work, and that it makes citizens *comfortable* rather than afraid. Arendt's account in *The Origins of Totalitarianism* (1951) is different and darker: atomisation plus terror plus an all-explaining fiction that must be internally consistent rather than true. Both descriptions fit the evidence. Which one you adopt changes what you look for.

📄 **Primary source.** Speer's own account of the Cathedral of Light in *Inside the Third Reich*. Read it knowing Speer was a self-interested narrator.
