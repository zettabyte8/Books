## A note on this collaboration

This book was made by three parties. Thomas G. Coleman designed the inquiry and wrote every prompt. Gemini produced the first draft across a single 2026 session of fifteen prompts. Venice AI then audited that draft, identified thirteen classes of defect — missing pedagogy, an unusable reading level, absent case studies, four contested findings stated as settled — and rebuilt it.

The first edition was also provincial: European and American throughout, with the rest of the world appearing only as atrocity or as colour. Edition 2.2 fixes that properly. Three chapters now run the analysis through the Gulf, Latin America, South Asia, and Africa, with sources from those regions' own scholarship rather than wire copy. One chapter would have been a correction; three is a claim about where the field's real cases are.

Nothing here is hidden. Appendix C reproduces the original prompt sequence in full, annotated, so a student can see exactly how the argument was assembled and where a machine was guessing. Where the audit found the first draft wrong, this edition says so in the open, in a **Contested** box. That transparency is not decoration. It is the book's own thesis applied to the book.

Readers should treat every citation in Appendix D as a lead to verify, not as a guarantee.

## How to use this book

Each chapter runs the same sequence: **objectives, key terms, text, primary source, contested findings where relevant, comprehension checks, one activity, summary.** The apparatus is not padding. Reading objectives before the text roughly doubles retention; the checks test whether you actually followed the argument; the activities move you from knowing to doing.

**Three reading routes exist.**

- **The short course (eight sessions):** Chapters 1, 2, 3, 4, 12, 13, 14, 15.
- **The full course (a term):** all fifteen, in order, with Appendix E.
- **The reference route:** Chapters 1–4 as a toolkit, then whichever case study you need.

**Symbols.** 🔧 Activity. ⚠️ Contested. 📄 Primary source. → Cross-reference.

## Note to educators

Every chapter carries objectives written to be copyable into a lesson plan. Appendix E holds a full scheme of work, answer key, and assessment rubrics. The book is deliberately structured so a class can run Chapter 13 (defence) in week two and use it against Chapters 6–12 for the rest of the term.

Chapters 9 to 11 exist because Parts I and II are otherwise Atlantic. Teach them as a block, in order, immediately after Chapter 8: the comparison across all four is the point, and running only one of them reintroduces the imbalance it was written to fix.

Sensitive material appears in Chapter 6 (atrocity propaganda, genocide) and Chapter 8 (Rwanda). Both are handled without assuming prior knowledge.

## Conventions

**Light and dark** are analytic poles, not moral verdicts. Plenty of campaigns are both.

**"Mass"** means a population large enough that no individual can verify the claims being made about it — roughly, more people than you can know personally. The threshold is practical, not numerical.

**Contested boxes** mark findings where competent researchers disagree. They are the most important pages in the book. A text that never says "we don't know" is teaching you to trust it too much.
